Privacy

Ledger is a private finance app that one family runs on a computer in their home and shares by invitation. This page says what information it handles and what happens to it. Last changed November 18, 2026.

If you ask for a copy

The request page asks for your name and email address. They are kept so the person who invited you can recognise you, approve or decline the request, and send you your link. They are not used for anything else. The page also uses a check from Cloudflare that a person, not a program, is filling it in; that check looks at your browser, not at what you type.

Signing in with Google or an emailed code

Each household's copy sits behind a sign-in check run by Cloudflare. You can pass it with a code emailed to you or with your Google account.

  • If you choose Google, Google tells Cloudflare your email address and basic profile (your name and picture) so it can confirm who you are.
  • The only thing checked is whether your email address is on the list of people invited to that copy.
  • Ledger does not receive your Google password, and it does not read your Gmail, contacts, calendar, files or anything else in your Google account. It asks Google for nothing beyond your identity.
  • Your Google information is not sold, not shared with anyone else, not used for advertising, and not used to train AI.

What you put in your copy

  • Your accounts, transactions, budgets, bills, goals, any allowances you record for your children (their first names and the amounts), investment holdings, tax figures, any receipt pictures you choose to keep and, only if you switch that on, the tax documents you add (which can carry Social Security and account numbers) are stored in your household's own database on the home server of the person who invited you. It is separate from every other household's.
  • That person looks after the server and its backups. They can see when your copy was last used, whether setup was finished, whether a bank is connected, how many devices are signed in, and what its AI features have cost. Ledger does not show them what is in it: not your balances, purchases, budgets or the questions you ask. As with any service, the person who runs the server could in principle open its files, so use a copy only if you trust them with that. Your copy also keeps the list of email addresses approved to open it.
  • If you set a Ledger password, it is stored only in scrambled form. Nobody can read it back. If you choose to open your copy with the sign-in check alone, Ledger confirms Cloudflare's signed note about who you are on each sign-in and keeps only your email address beside that device's sign-in. If you give another adult a sign-in of their own, their name is kept with it.

Services Ledger uses on your behalf

  • Bank connection (optional). If you connect a bank, it goes through SimpleFIN Bridge, a separate service you sign up for yourself. Your bank password is typed on their site and never reaches Ledger. The connection is read-only.
  • Home Assistant sensors (optional, only where Ledger runs inside Home Assistant). Off unless you switch them on, one at a time. A sensor you switch on is written to your own Home Assistant, where everyone who uses it can see it and where it is kept in its history: three say only yes or no (a bill due tomorrow, checking forecast to run low, a budget over), one is a number of days, and two are figures (what is left before payday, and the share of the budget used). Nothing goes anywhere else.
  • AI features (optional). The written recap, Ask your CFO and the readers for receipts, pay stubs and pay calendars send what each job needs to Claude, made by Anthropic: for example a month's figures, or the picture you chose. Anthropic states that information sent this way is not used to train its models. You can switch these features off. Where Ledger runs inside Home Assistant, a household can choose the AI it has already set up there instead; the month's figures, shop names and questions then go to that AI, whichever company or machine it is, and not to Anthropic. A household can switch on "Hide names from the AI", which replaces the names of its people, employers, accounts and banks with plain labels in what the recap and Ask your CFO are sent.
  • A home's value (optional). A property you add is kept at a value you type, and nothing is sent anywhere for it. If you choose "Look it up each month" for a home, that home's address and your own RentCast key are sent to RentCast, a property data service, once a month and when you press "Check now", and the address is kept in your copy so it can ask. It is off unless you set it up, and switching it off forgets the address.
  • A vehicle's VIN (optional). When adding a vehicle you can press "Fill these in from the VIN". That sends the VIN you type, and nothing else, to the U.S. National Highway Traffic Safety Administration's free decoder, which returns the year, make, model and type. Ledger does not keep the VIN. If you never press it, nothing is sent.
  • A booking calendar (optional). If you tell Ledger a side job is paid by booked sessions and give it your calendar's private iCal address, Ledger reads that calendar every few hours to work out the pay to expect. It keeps the address, and the times and titles of the events it finds, in your household's database. It never changes the calendar and sends it nothing.
  • Logos. To show a shop's logo, Ledger looks up the shop's website name with Logo.dev, Google's icon service or DuckDuckGo's. Only the website name is sent, never a purchase or an amount.
  • Investment prices (optional, off unless you switch it on). If you list investments and turn on price look-ups, Ledger asks Yahoo Finance for the price of each ticker symbol you hold. Only the symbols are sent, never how many shares you have, what they are worth, or who you are.
  • Checking for a newer version (only where you run Ledger yourself with Docker). Once a day such a copy reads the latest version number and the list of changes from Ledger's public project page on GitHub. Nothing about your household is sent; GitHub sees that computer's internet address, as it did when Ledger was installed. It can be switched off in Settings, "Updates". A copy someone hosts for you, or one inside Home Assistant, does not do this.
  • Email. Messages such as your welcome email or a password reset code are sent from the inviting person's own email account.

What Ledger does not do

  • It has no advertising and no tracking or analytics from outside companies.
  • It does not sell or rent anyone's information.
  • It cannot move money. It only reads and records.

Keeping, correcting and deleting

You can download every transaction from Settings in your copy at any time. You can delete your copy and everything in it yourself, at any time, from Settings in your copy ("Delete everything"); the person who runs the server is told only that it is now empty. To have a request removed, ask the person who invited you. Backups of the server that include your copy age out within about a month.

Children

Ledger is for adults managing a household's money. It is not offered to children.
